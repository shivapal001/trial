01 // About the game

      
A district that does not want you to leave


      

        

          

            PREY: THE ESCAPE is a single-player, offline first-person horror game
            for Android. You start with no weapon and no explanation — only a dark stretch of
            streets, shuttered buildings and a way out that is firmly locked.
          

          

            Progress means paying attention: where the light falls, where the patrols go, which
            lock takes which key. Every level is a self-contained escape — cross it, and the game
            remembers how far you got.

02 // Story

      
You woke up in the wrong place.


      

        

          Cold concrete. A lamp that buzzes and dies. No voices, no traffic — only your own
          footsteps answering themselves down the alley.
        

        

          The district is a maze of service lanes, fences and gated passages. Some gates open
          with a turn; others want a key you have not found yet, and at least one wants two.
          Somewhere behind you, something has started to move.
        

        

          The game opens with its text crawl, then hands you the street. From there the story is
          told by what you find: the locks that were shut from the inside, the routes that were
          blocked on purpose, and the exit that keeps getting further away.
        

        
Find the keys. Open the gates. Get out.

05 // Gameplay

      
How a night here plays out


      

        

          

            You move in first person with a left-thumb joystick and drag anywhere on the right to
            look. Interact, jump, crouch, sprint, inventory and pause sit within reach of both
            thumbs.
          

          

            The loop is simple and tense: listen, watch, move. Patrols give away
            their position; darkness hides you but hides the path too. When a route is barred you
            are hunting for the key that matches it — sometimes two keys for one panel.
          

          

            Getting caught sends you back to the start of the level. Reaching the exit moves the
            story forward and stores your progress.
          

          

            Tuned for short sessions on a phone: levels are compact, the interface is touch-first,
            and graphics presets let older devices keep up.
